# Grading System — Pentecost University

## Graduate School grading scale
The PUC Graduate School grades courses on a marks-to-letter-grade scale:

| Marks (%) | Letter grade | Grade point | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80–100 | A | 4.00 | Excellent |
| 70–79 | B+ | 3.50 | Very Good |
| 60–69 | B | 2.50 | Good |
| 50–59 | C | 2.00 | Pass |
| 30–49 | D | 1.50 | Fail |
| 0–29 | F | 1.00 | Fail |

Special (non-numeric) grades:
- **Z** — Disqualification (awarded only by the Academic Board, for examination
  malpractice; may bar a candidate from University examinations for a period
  or indefinitely, or lead to expulsion).
- **I** — Incomplete with justification (an acceptable reason, e.g. illness).
  The student must complete the course the next time it runs; if not
  rectified it converts to an F on the transcript.
- **Y** — Continuing (a course that runs into the next tri/semester).
- **X** — Incomplete without justification (an unacceptable reason).

**Pass grades:** A, B+, B, C. **Failure grades:** D, F, X, Z.

## GPA, CGPA and FGPA (Graduate School)
- **Grade Point (GP):** credit hours for the course × the grade point for the
  grade earned.
- **GPA:** total grade points earned in a period ÷ total credits attempted in
  that period. All courses attempted count, whether passed or failed.
- **CGPA (Cumulative GPA):** total grade points earned up to any point in the
  programme ÷ total credits registered for up to that point.
- **FGPA (Final GPA):** the CGPA calculated at the end of the whole
  programme — this is what determines eligibility for the degree, not the
  running CGPA.

Worked example (from the Graduate School Policies): a student takes 6
courses worth 3 credit hours each (18 credits total) and earns grade points
totalling 57.0 across them; GPA = 57.0 ÷ 18 = **3.17**.

## Standing, warnings and eligibility (Graduate School)
- A graduate student is in **good standing** with a CGPA of 2.50 or higher.
  Falling below that leads to withdrawal.
- An **academic warning** (noted on the transcript) is issued after two
  grades below C, and for any CGPA drop under 2.50.
- Candidates who finish coursework with an **FGPA below 2.50** are deemed to
  have failed the programme, even if every individual course was passed.
- A grade below C requires the course to be re-registered and re-sat the
  next time it is offered — there is no special standalone re-sit sitting.

## Grade appeals
To dispute a grade: raise it with the course lecturer first; if unresolved,
escalate to the Programme Coordinator; if still unresolved, submit a formal
"Grade Review Form" to the Dean's Office.

## Undergraduate examinations — eligibility and integrity
Current rules for undergraduate end-of-semester examinations (2021/22
Examination Guide):
- Only students who **attended lectures and completed coursework**, and who
  have **fully paid their fees**, are eligible to sit an exam. Fees can still
  be settled up to 30 minutes before the exam starts; after that, no entry.
- A valid Student ID card is required for entry; no other ID is accepted.
- Arriving more than 30 minutes after the start time means no admission to
  the hall at all; arriving within that window means no extra time is given.
- Confirmed examination malpractice can lead to the paper's marks being
  cancelled and a grade **F substituted** for that paper — the only way to
  recover is to retake the course from scratch (register and attend lectures
  again). More serious or repeated cases can lead to an F for the whole
  semester's exams, being barred from University examinations for a set
  period, suspension, or dismissal.
