# Student Bursary — Pentecost University

A bursary is a one-off monetary award, decided each year by the Scholarship
Committee, that goes towards a student's fees. Unlike the Full and Half
Scholarships, a bursary is not a fixed percentage of tuition — the amount
is set case by case based on the student's need and the funds available for
that year. A bursary can be applied to tuition or to non-tuition charges
such as the Technology User fee or accommodation.

## Who is eligible
- Continuing students who have completed at least one semester.
- Evidence of financial hardship that has arisen or worsened during study —
  for example loss of a parent or guardian, a family member's serious
  illness, or loss of family income.
- Cumulative GPA of at least 2.5 and no unresolved disciplinary case.
- Priority is given to students who are at risk of dropping out for
  purely financial reasons and who are close to completing their programme.

## Required documents
- Latest academic transcript.
- A detailed hardship statement (maximum 400 words).
- Evidence of the hardship: medical report, death certificate, employer
  redundancy letter, or a letter from a church or community leader who
  knows the family's situation.
- A recent statement of the student's fee balance from the Finance Office.
- Ghana Card or passport.

## How to apply
1. Collect or download the bursary request form from the Finance Office or
   the "Financial Aid / Scholarship" section of the e-portal.
2. Attach the supporting evidence and the fee-balance statement.
3. Submit to the Finance Office. Bursary requests can be submitted at any
   time but are reviewed in batches, usually once per semester and again
   before examinations.
4. Urgent cases (a student about to be deregistered for non-payment) can
   ask the Dean of Students to refer the request for an out-of-cycle review.

## After the award
- The amount is credited directly to the student's fee account; it is not
  paid to the student in cash.
- A bursary is for the semester or year it is granted and does not renew
  automatically — a student must reapply if the hardship continues.
